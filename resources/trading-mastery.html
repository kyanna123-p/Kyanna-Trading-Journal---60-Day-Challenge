Market Behavior

Trade the instrument, not the indicator
Price action is the primary evidence. Indicators describe price; they are not price. Too many indicators hide the underlying market behavior. Exact tops and bottoms are not realistic objectives.
The three states
Narrow State / Trap Zone: price and MAs compress, with potential for expansion. Wide State: price has separated from the averages; reversion becomes a possibility. Trending State: directional movement with successive legs and pauses. A market state is not itself an entry trigger.
Seven key trading positions
From central Trap Zone: bullish +1, +2, +3 and bearish -1, -2, -3. Position 1 is the preferred early directional entry; Position 3 is the three-finger spread, where an optional countertrend entry requires reversal indication. Preserve the lesson's exact candle-color diagrams as source material.
Compression versus extension
A tight, narrow relationship between price and moving averages can precede an explosive expansion. A very wide separation, especially a three-finger spread at Position 3, can precede a pullback. Neither is a certainty.
The three bullish pause types
Type 1: a distinct pullback then possible continuation. Type 2: sideways shelf or horizontal consolidation then possible continuation. Type 3: a shallow, brief hesitation within a strong move. The pause forms are distinct from candle-count illustrations.
The three bearish pause types
Type 1: an upward retracement within a downtrend. Type 2: a sideways shelf. Type 3: brief hesitation with little recovery. They may precede a further drop, but continuation must not be presumed.
Five-leg market advance
A trend can develop through 3–5 legs with different pauses separating each leg. In the lesson: Leg 1 BUY; Leg 2 BUY; Leg 3 RIDE; Leg 4 TAKE PROFIT; Leg 5 TAKE PROFIT. A mature run is more vulnerable to exhaustion, not guaranteed to reverse.
Four characteristics of market tops
1. Dynamic 3–5 leg run. 2. Near-vertical acceleration, often in a late leg. 3. Final bar of the run-up is the biggest. 4. Three-finger spread / dual space. These often stack, but one can be enough to warrant caution. ‘Dual space’ still needs its precise source definition.
Odds of a new high after a pullback
Lesson's claimed probabilities by where the pullback ends within the prior rise: upper third 80%; middle third 65%; lower third 35%; below lower third 15%. These are instructor estimates, not independently validated probabilities. Distinguish the reversal color change ending a pullback from its first contrary candle.
Three characteristics of a healthy pullback
No violence (controlled selling), does not break the mid-zone of the previous bullish move, and forms approximately a 45° visual retracement. Angle depends on chart scaling. Preserve all three characteristics without replacing them with arbitrary code thresholds.
Sharp Drop + Time (T = S)
Following a rapid decline, an early fake-out rally can fail. Extended consolidation near the lows may provide time for stabilization and a possible new directional move. ‘Time = Space’ is an instructional heuristic, not an equation proving a reversal.

Moving Averages

Timeframe hierarchy
Daily: check for chop and assess daily MAs; 4-hour: key support/resistance levels; 1-hour: major context and potential explosive moves; 15-minute: intraday trend; 5-minute: primary entry/confirmation; 2-minute: optional micro refinement, not the initial scanner timeframe.
Your selected moving averages
200 / 50 / 20 / 10. The 200 is most powerful when flat as a potential support/resistance reference; the 20 is most powerful when visibly trending around 45°; 50 provides intermediate/daily context; 10 guides very fast movements. Distinguish these from the instructor's alternate 8/13 settings.
Four principles for MA-based entries
1. The 200-period moving average offers the best support and resistance qualities when it is flat. 2. The 20-period moving average performs best as support or resistance when the market is trending up or down. 3. A solid market trader ensures that most of their long or short entries are at or near these key moving averages. 4. It's not necessary all the time, but being near the 20 or 200-period moving averages will radically improve your trading.
Slope plus price location
Always go with the flow of the MAs. Evaluate whether averages are rising, falling or flat, and whether price is above, below, between, or repeatedly crossing them. Favor 200 below price for longs and 200 above price for shorts; these are preferences, not absolute requirements.
20 MA squeeze
A rising 20 SMA produces higher lows beneath a flat 200 SMA. Price repeatedly tests the 200 from below and compresses into narrowing space, sometimes breaking upward. A breakout is illustrative rather than inevitable.
20 SMA crossing below 200 SMA
The lesson's chart depicts a declining 20 SMA crossing beneath a flatter 200 SMA followed by bearish movement and later recovery. Treat the crossover as price-derived context, not a guaranteed directional signal.
Trifecta
A surge away from the 200 MA on all three charts: 2-minute, 5-minute and 15-minute. No claim that the surges must begin on the exact same tick; precise timing remains to be specified.
Fast trend MA warning versus MA exit
A full bar entirely below the fast 10 MA on a lower timeframe warns that a bullish move may have topped out. Separately, the confirmed 2-bar MA break exit requires two consecutive candle CLOSES beyond the chosen MA: below for longs, above for shorts. Fast trends may follow 10 MA; steadier trends 20 MA.

Trading Events & 180 Family

Bull and Bear Elephant Bars
Very substantial directional real body compared with recent candles; lesson compares body size against approximately 70% of the previous 20 bodies. An Elephant Bar indicates unusual pressure, not proof of institutional identities. The instructor claims roughly 80% continuation for a Bear Elephant Bar; this is unverified.
Bull Elephant intrabar entry
For a developing 2-minute Bull Elephant Bar, the instructor's anticipation timing is when roughly 1:30 remains (30 seconds after the candle opens). This is an aggressive unconfirmed entry concept, not the default 5-minute scanner rule. Stop reference below the signal bar.
Five-bar breakout indication
Out of the Narrow State, a solid ELEPHANT-SIZED bearish candle must clear the lowest low of the preceding five bars to indicate a possible -1 breakout. Size and five-bar low break are both necessary to the taught bearish indication.
Bottoming Tail Bar
Lower wick is 80–85% of the total bar in the user's preferred example (instructor also showed two-thirds minimum). Body may be green, red or doji. Potential buy above the bar's high; protective stop below its low. Signals lower-price rejection, not necessarily guaranteed reversal.
Color change versus power event
A simple red-to-green change is not automatically a Bull 180. A 180 requires a powerful opposing bar and meaningful reversal of the prior candle. Preserve comparative visual examples. An optional add is on the FIRST color change after the initial directional entry, if the setup remains valid.
Standard Bull 180
Two substantial bars: red followed by strong green reversal, preferably in a meaningful location. Standard stock entry: $0.01 above the preceding RED bar's HIGH, not the later green high; futures use applicable ticks. Stop reference below pattern low. The lesson specifies the 2-bar stop rule does not apply to this event.
Standard Bear 180
Two substantial bars: green followed by red that overcomes preceding bullish movement. Standard short trigger below the preceding GREEN bar's LOW. Protective stop reference above the pattern high. Distinguish a true Bear 180 from a simple color change.
Anticipation Bull 180 — special qualification
ONLY when the preceding red candle is a BEAR ELEPHANT BAR may the developing green candle trigger an earlier buy at 80% recovery of that red bar's high-low range, BEFORE the red high is broken. Anticipation level = red low + 0.8 × (red high − red low). Otherwise use the standard 180 trigger.
Anticipation risk and stops
Earlier 80% entry can reduce price-to-stop distance for the same fixed stop and position size, but enters before confirmation. Preserve both illustrated stop references: tighter bottom-third stop and ideal stop below the entire two-bar pattern; evaluate total risk for each.
180 nullifying an Elephant Bar
Instructor describes an opposing 180 that defeats an Elephant Bar as producing longer/stronger follow-through than ordinary continuation. The claim is qualitative, not validated performance data. Bull and Bear 180 were called the most profitable events in the lesson, also an instructor claim.
Four ideal locations for Bull 180
1. Far below declining 20 SMA after an extended drop. 2. At a rising 20 SMA during a pullback. 3. Above a FLAT 200 SMA as support. 4. Breakout above a FLAT 200 SMA after repeated tests. The last two specifically require illustrating the flat 200.

Execution & Risk

Preferred entry logic
Primary entries at Position +1/-1 after Narrow State and qualified indication; optional countertrend entry at extended Position +3/-3 requires genuine reversal evidence. Favor longs with 200 MA below price and shorts with 200 MA above price. Trade price action rather than indicators.
First color change optional add
After initial entry, the FIRST opposite-color candle may provide an optional position add, as taught. Do not treat every color change as an add signal. Consider position exposure and stop distance.
Profit taking: two or three pushes
The putting-it-together lesson lists a 2–3 push profit-taking method; the Elephant Power example shows taking profit after three pushes, moving protection to breakeven and optionally holding a runner.
Profit taking: new high/low
The second exit framework is taking profit at a new directional high for a long or new directional low for a short. This is distinct from the 2–3 pushes framework.
Profit taking: stop-out
A stop out can be a NEW protective stop moved INTO PROFIT being broken, or a break of the respected moving average. For your MA-close rule, use two consecutive closes across the selected MA. A stop out is not necessarily a loss.
Types of trading
Wealth/investing: years; Core: months; Swing: days–weeks; Day: intraday; Scalping: seconds–minutes. Using a lower timeframe to enter does not by itself define the holding style.
